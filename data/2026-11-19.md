Our technique prevents invariant violations, state explosion, and false rejections while incurring negligible (1.48%) overhead across Linux kernel selftests and production suites such as Cilium. Motivation: SAC 系统（Corfu、NOPaxos、LazyLog、SwitchBFT 等）十多年来仅靠纸笔证明或有界模型检验论证正确性；而将线性化点放在 sequencer 上的常规证明方法因视图切换可能丢弃请求而失效。

Method: 扩展 prophecy variables 以处理视图切换，提出跨视图 prophecy peek（cross-view prophecy peek），使证明能跟踪请求跨越视图切换直到其被提交或永久丢弃；并将该框架应用于 NOPaxos、Corfu、LazyLog 的具体实现。

Result: 发现现有 SAC 实现中的 bug（含一个会丢失已提交请求的 bug）并修复且证明修复正确；构造出带机器检验线性一致性证明的验证实现，性能与开源未验证版本相差不超过 17%。

Conclusion: 形式化验证 SAC 协议实现是可行的，跨视图 prophecy peek 是应对视图切换挑战的关键技术；验证实现与证明将开源。

Abstract: Many distributed systems achieve high performance by relying on dedicated sequencers to order requests, leaving replicas to commit them in that order. Despite more than a decade of such sequencer-aided coordination (SAC) systems, from Corfu and NOPaxos to LazyLog and SwitchBFT, their correctness has been argued only with pen-and-paper proofs or bounded model checking at the protocol level.
  In this paper, we formally verify implementations of SAC protocols. The sequencer seems to make this easy: it decides the order of all requests, so the proof can place each request's linearization point at the sequencer using a well-known technique, prophecy variables. Yet a linearization point placed at the sequencer is not durable, because the sequencer orders a request before any replica commits it. Crashes and other rare events may trigger a view change that discards the request, nullifying the earlier decision and the proof that rests on it.
  Our framework, Stephen, extends prophecy variables to handle view changes, and we make three contributions. (1) We develop cross-view prophecy peek, which lets the proof follow a request across view changes until it is either committed or permanently discarded. (2) Our verification uncovered bugs in existing SAC implementations, including one that can lose committed requests. We fix them and prove the fixes correct. (3) We apply Stephen to NOPaxos, Corfu and LazyLog. We build verified implementations with machine-checked proofs of linearizability. They perform within 17% of their open-source, unverified counterparts. The verified implementations and proofs will be open-sourced.
